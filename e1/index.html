What the E1 has to prove

The stance survives

The car sits the way a donk is supposed to sit when it's done. If the conversion changes the stance, the conversion failed.

The pack goes low

Battery mass between the rails and under the floor — lowering the center of mass instead of adding to the problem.

Brakes match the wheels

Stopping power specified against the actual rolling diameter, with regen carrying the everyday load.

It drives like a car, not a science project

Range, charging, heat, and 12V accessories all handled. Turn the key equivalent, and go.

It's repeatable

Every part of the E1 that works becomes a module in the E-Box. Every part that doesn't becomes a note.

  4. Phase 4TBD

    Brakes, power electronics, charging

    Brake package sized to the wheels, inverter and DC-DC placement, onboard charger, port location, HV safety.

  5. Phase 5TBD

    Cooling, HVAC, 12V

    The unglamorous half. Loops, radiators, cabin heat and A/C without an engine to borrow from, and the whole low-voltage side.

    Body, paint, interior

    Radiused fenders, bodywork, candy, glass, stitching, and the system. The part everybody photographs.

  7. Phase 7TBD

    Shakedown

    Road miles, thermal testing, brake testing, range validation, and the list of things to change on the next one.

Battery platform

The E-Box platform's Tier L pack uses EV West Tesla Model S 18650 modules — 16 modules, 96s, 84.8 kWh gross at a 364.8V nominal pack voltage. Proven, salvage-sourced cells rather than a custom pack, which keeps cost and lead time down for a low-volume build.

Classic Soul. Electric Power.

EDONKS electrifies old-school big-wheel cars — donks, boxes, and bubbles — without erasing the culture that made them. Born in Florida and built by a rider, EDONKS started with the E1, founder EJ's own electric donk, and grew into the E-Box: a modular electric chassis platform purpose-built for the physics of big wheels.
