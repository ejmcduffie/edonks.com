How It Works

Every EDONKS build follows the same process, whether it's the E1 or yours. Eight steps, no shortcuts.

  1. 01

    Choose Your Car

    Donk, box, bubble, or something else on big wheels. Tell us what you're riding and what shape it's in — runs, rolls, or parked under a cover.

  2. 02

    Inspect the Chassis

    Frame condition, rust, ride height, and the wheel package get measured and documented before anything is designed.

  3. 03

    Engineer E-Box Config

    Battery tier, drive trim, and mount points get specified against your car's body style and the stance you want to keep.

  4. 04

    Install Battery + Powertrain

    Pack goes low and centered between the rails. Motor, reduction, and mounts go in next, placed for balance.

  5. 05

    Tune Suspension + Stance

    Ride height, rake, and geometry get set for the wheel package — nose up, tail down, the way a donk is supposed to sit.

  6. 06

    Integrate Controls

    Inverter, DC-DC, onboard charger, and the low-voltage system that runs your lights, locks, and audio.

  7. 07

    Road Test

    Real miles. Thermal checks, brake checks, range validation, and a list of anything that needs another pass.

  8. 08

    Deliver Your Electric Donk

    Same stance. Same soul. New current. You drive it home.

Straight answers

Before you call

The questions every rider asks first. Where we don't have a real number yet, we say so — a guess on a website is worse than no answer.

When can my build start?

+

The E1 comes first. It's EJ's own car and the proof the process works. Rider builds follow once it's built and tested. Send your car's details now and you'll be in line when build slots open — dated updates on the E1 are coming as the build moves.

How long does a conversion take?

+

We don't have an honest number yet, so we're not posting one. The E1 is the build that sets the real timeline, and it gets documented step by step so the next estimate comes from a real car, not a guess. When we scope your car, you get a timeline for your car.

What does it cost?

+

Every build is quoted per car. We don't publish a price yet. What moves the number: the shape your car is in, battery size, drive setup, the wheel and brake package, and whether paint, interior, and audio are part of the job. Put your budget range on the form and we'll be straight with you about what's realistic.

How does my car get to you?

+

We're based in Florida. Whether you drive it in, trailer it, or arrange transport gets worked out on your first call, depending on where you are and whether the car runs. A non-running car is fine to start the conversation — the E1's donor car doesn't run either.

What do you look for when you inspect my car?

+

Frame and rust first — the frame carries the battery, so it has to be sound. Then we measure: frame rail spacing and height where the pack goes, body mount points, ride height, the wheel and tire package, and corner weights on scales before anything comes off the car. If the frame can't carry a pack safely, you hear that before any work starts.

Does my car need a new title or registration?

+

In Florida, a conversion changes the drivetrain, not the car: it keeps its VIN and its title, and Florida doesn't require state safety inspections or emissions testing for passenger cars. Exactly how the fuel type gets recorded after a conversion is something we're confirming with the state before the first customer car. Other states handle it differently, and we'll go over yours with you. This is general information, not legal advice.

Is there a warranty?

+

Warranty terms are being written now. They'll spell out what's covered, for how long, and where our work ends and your car's original parts begin, and you'll have them in writing before you commit to a build. We won't promise coverage on a website before it exists on paper.

How do I charge it?

+

The platform is designed around the two plugs public chargers use: J1772 for Level 2 AC charging at home or around town, and CCS for DC fast charging, so the car can drive to the show instead of riding a trailer. Charging speeds are design targets until the E1 proves them on the road. Home charging gets covered when we scope your build.

Where do you work?

+

EDONKS is based in Florida, and the first builds happen here. If you're outside Florida, reach out anyway — tell us where you are and we'll be honest about whether we can take it on yet. Partner shops in other states are part of the long-term plan, after the platform is proven.

What do I get at delivery?

+

Your car, same stance, new drivetrain. With it, the E-Box spec calls for a written emergency response sheet for your specific car (where the high-voltage disconnect is and what first responders should and shouldn't cut), one copy for you and one kept in the car; written confirmation that your wheel and tire setup is load-rated for the car's weight; and the records from your car's high-voltage safety tests. Final paperwork is confirmed in your build agreement.
